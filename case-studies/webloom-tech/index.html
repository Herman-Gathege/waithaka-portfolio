01

Overview

Webloom Tech Kenya builds websites, software and AI-assisted solutions for businesses. I joined as a Front-End Developer in June 2025 and work across client projects — e-commerce storefronts, creative services sites and personal branding work — as part of an agile delivery team.

The engagement is continuous rather than project-shaped: I move between client builds, front-end standards for the team, and the support that follows a launch.

02

What the work involves

  • Designing and delivering SEO-optimized front-end applications, with responsive, user-focused experiences as the baseline.
  • Architecting responsive, cross-browser compatible React.js, Vue.js and Nuxt.js applications with mobile-first design.
  • Consuming RESTful APIs to display data, validate input and post updates across client interfaces.
  • Working closely with UI/UX design partners, prototyping and testing design feasibility while acting as user advocate.
  • Acting as an internal reference point for front-end best practices and maintaining brand consistency across releases.
  • Reviewing peers’ code, adhering to change control procedures and delivering projects from conception to completion.
  • Leading client onboarding, post-launch support and development lifecycle documentation.

03

Approach

  • Mobile first, then widen: layouts are designed for the smallest screens and the constraints they impose, then extended for tablet and desktop so nothing is retrofitted.
  • Component reuse over one-off pages: shared components and patterns keep client builds consistent and keep later changes cheap.
  • API contracts early: agreeing what the interface sends and expects before styling avoids rework once real data arrives.
  • Design partnership: prototyping with designers during the concept stage surfaces feasibility issues while they are still design decisions rather than build problems.
  • Support after launch: onboarding and lifecycle documentation so clients and teammates can keep the product moving.

04

Where it stands

The engagement is ongoing, and client work continues across the agency’s service lines. Individual client sites and their performance figures are covered by client agreements, so this case study describes the practice and the scope of the role rather than claiming outcomes that are not mine to publish.

If you want detail on a specific type of build — an e-commerce front end, a Nuxt marketing site, an API-heavy dashboard — I’m happy to walk through it directly.

07

Key takeaways

  • Agency work rewards reusable foundations: the second client build is much faster when the first one left behind components instead of one-off pages.
  • Being the internal reference point for front-end practice is a different skill from building — it means documenting, reviewing and explaining decisions.
  • Post-launch support and onboarding are part of the product; treating documentation as a deliverable keeps the client relationship steady.
